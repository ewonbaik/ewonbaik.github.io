Dissertation (Book Project)

Politics in Translation: Immigrants’ Dual and Dynamic Political Preferences

My book project asks how people navigate political preferences between two countries. Challenging existing narratives of preference formation and immigrant incorporation, I reframe immigrants who arrive in a new country as carriers of dual and dynamic political identities, rather than political blank slates. In doing so, I recast immigrant incorporation as a process of strategic political adjustment rather than one-way assimilation. The book shows this via three pathways: (1) the varying portability of political preferences across national contexts, (2) the persistence of pre-migration political socialization after migration, and (3) the pressures that geopolitical rivalry places on immigrants’ political alignments. Together, these findings reveal political behavior as not merely a domestic phenomenon, but a transnational process shaped by comparative institutions and geopolitics.
